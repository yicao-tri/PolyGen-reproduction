# Results and Discussion: Interpolation benchmark

## Results

We first evaluated target control inside the frozen training support, using the same HTP–MD representation, verifier, decoding configuration, generation budget, and five matched seeds as the upper-tail benchmark. The requested targets were Q50 (−4.2653), Q65 (−4.1874), and Q80 (−4.0845) log10(S cm−1). Each arm produced 500 candidates per target grid point across the five seeds.

Continuous conditioning showed a more graded internal response than the binary control, although the frozen RF predictions remained substantially below the requested target curve. Mean strict-hit rates for continuous conditioning were 0.504, 0.532, and 0.550 at Q50, Q65, and Q80, respectively, compared with 0.486, 0.486, and 0.496 for binary conditioning. The corresponding target MAE values were 0.070, 0.109, and 0.166 log units for continuous conditioning and 0.076, 0.114, and 0.201 for binary conditioning. Averaged across the three targets, continuous conditioning improved strict-hit rate by 0.039 and reduced target MAE by 0.015 log units.

Validity was complete at the cell level for both arms, with no unsupported target cells. The mean validity fraction was 0.545 for continuous conditioning and 0.511 for binary conditioning. Continuous validity increased from 0.512 at Q50 to 0.578 at Q80, whereas binary validity remained approximately flat at 0.511–0.516. This difference is important for interpreting the heatmap: interpolation tests control within support without the unsupported-cell collapse observed in the upper-tail comparison.

The interpolation-specific controllability diagnostic was the candidate-wise monotonicity fraction. This is the proportion of valid seed-by-candidate trajectories whose predicted conductivity was non-decreasing from Q50 to Q65 to Q80. The fractions were 0.652, 0.639, 0.690, 0.743, and 0.656 for binary seeds 17, 42, 202, 303, and 907, and 0.656, 0.690, 0.576, 0.679, and 0.589 for continuous seeds 17, 42, 202, 303, and 907, respectively. The mean fractions were therefore similar (0.678 for binary and 0.638 for continuous). This result prevents an overclaim: continuous conditioning improved average target adherence, but did not make every individual candidate trajectory monotonic.

## One-to-one comparison with the upper-tail benchmark

The paired figures use the same visual grammar as the upper-tail publication set. Figure 1 compares RF response, target MAE, and validity; Figure 2 compares strict-hit rate and validity across cells; Figure 3 adds the interpolation-specific target-response and monotonicity diagnostics. The scientific interpretation differs by regime.

In interpolation, the requested targets lie within the support used to fit the generator and verifier. Therefore, a response curve that follows the requested target and a low target MAE are evidence of verifier-defined control. In the upper-tail experiment, targets approached or exceeded a Q80 training boundary, and the locked-tail RF audit showed a mean observed-minus-predicted residual of +0.262 log units. The upper-tail response therefore cannot be read as a simple failure of generation: the verifier itself underpredicted the locked region. Interpolation is consequently the appropriate regime for quantifying control precision, whereas upper-tail is the appropriate regime for testing whether that control survives a distribution shift.

The two regimes also differ in their failure pattern. All interpolation arm-by-target cells were supported and retained valid candidates. In upper-tail, binary and unconditional+rerranking lost support in distant target cells, while continuous conditioning retained the highest valid yield but produced no RF-confirmed candidates above the training maximum. Thus, the interpolation result supports a conditional statement—continuous conditioning improves surrogate-defined internal target adherence under this representation—while the upper-tail result limits any claim of extrapolation until independent MD evidence is available.

## Discussion

These results establish the first half of the interpolation-to-extrapolation benchmark. Continuous conditioning is not merely a relabeling of the binary high/low prompt: under matched seeds and generation budgets, it gives lower target error and higher strict-hit rate across the internal Q50–Q80 grid. The effect is modest in absolute magnitude, but it is directionally consistent across the three target levels and is accompanied by higher valid-output coverage.

The monotonicity analysis adds an important qualification. Continuous conditioning improves aggregate target adherence without guaranteeing candidate-level monotonicity. This distinction matters because average response curves can conceal stochastic trajectory crossings. The appropriate interpretation is therefore improved population-level controllability, not deterministic ordering of every generated structure.

The interpolation benchmark also clarifies why upper-tail validation must remain separate. Within-support success can be measured against the requested target using the frozen RF, but the observed response curve shows that even interpolation is not perfect target realization: the verifier predictions remain compressed near the training distribution. Beyond-support performance is further confounded by surrogate calibration and support. The locked-tail RF audit demonstrates that high apparent performance cannot be established by asking the same verifier to both rank candidates and certify extrapolation. MD should consequently be used as an independent endpoint for the upper-tail candidate queue, with uncertainty, novelty, support, and failure strata preserved.

The defensible claim boundary is: **under the frozen HTP–MD representation and surrogate, continuous conductivity conditioning improves verifier-defined interpolation control across Q50–Q80, while the paired upper-tail benchmark provides no surrogate-confirmed evidence of crossing the training frontier.** Neither result is an MD-confirmed or experimentally measured conductivity claim.
